# ADR 0020: Lordship: tenure, influence, edicts and fallout

Date: 2026-10-13 (revised the same day after a review against the code) · Status: Proposed. The design awaits your approval with [LORDSHIP_DESIGN.md](../LORDSHIP_DESIGN.md); this record moves to Accepted then, and gains its "as built" notes during M4a ([M4A_PLAN.md](../M4A_PLAN.md)) · Requirements: GP-31, GP-32, GP-33, GP-34, SIM-60, SIM-61, SIM-62, SIM-63, SIM-64, UI-11, UI-12, RND-12, NFR-11 (also GP-17, GP-22, SIM-24). Amends [ADR 0013](0013-runs-presets-and-run-config.md) (the rival's defence follows its realm), [ADR 0014](0014-ownership-conquest-and-loyalty.md), [ADR 0015](0015-monuments-and-faction-modifiers.md) and [ADR 0018](0018-village-food-in-a-run.md), and BUILD_GUIDE D2 (`docs/m4/BUILD_GUIDE.md`).

## Context

The M4 playtest's first verdict (2026-10-13): "the game feels like we are running around taking points like king of the hill but my actions feel unweighted." In the M4 build a settlement is a flag: `sim_agents::set_owner` flips `Owner` and reseeds loyalty, and nothing a villager does depends on who owns the town. There is one way to take a town (an `Attack` and a two-hour hold), no command aimed at a town, no visible reason for a town's mood, and an economy that makes almost no weapons. You decided (2026-10-13) to add M4a "Lordship" before M4c: ways to take a town with lasting states, influence that spreads from holdings, fallout from conquest in both directions, and governing towns through edicts, buildings and stewards.

Constraints:
- Ownership changes only in `conquest::transfer_seeded`; goodwill only through `GoodwillLedger` deeds; `FactionModifiers` only in `rebuild_modifiers`; goods between two stockpiles only in the army's logistics step (`orders::run_orders`).
- Run-only rules leave the developed world, the skirmish and `--battle` byte-identical (ADR 0013), and draw from new streams.
- Progress-driver steps are appended, never reordered.
- Saved enums are append only; every hashed type is saved.

## Decision

1. **Tenure.** A run settlement carries `Tenure` (how it was taken, by whom, since when, its scar, blood, promises kept and broken, an agent's stir, a ring of past takings). `transfer_seeded` writes it from the `Cause`, so the single chokepoint for ownership also records how. New causes, appended: `Sack`, `Submission`, `Subversion`, `Defiance`, `Fallout`, `Reprisal`, `Edict`, `Caught`, `Deposed`. `transfer_seeded` maps them to event kinds and counters explicitly (Sack is a conquest; Submission and Subversion are takes with their own kinds; Defiance reads like a revolt), because today it files every cause but two under `Conquest`.
2. **Itemised loyalty.** `LoyaltyTerms` (resentment, awe, contest, bond, dues, table, steward, stirred) are rebuilt hourly by a progress step and added to `loyalty_target` as one `bias` argument. Absent outside a run, the bias is `0.0`, an exact no-op. `DeedLog` keeps the last eight goodwill changes per town, written by `apply_goodwill`, so the panel can say why.
3. **Four ways to take a town,** each ending in `transfer_seeded`: the existing `Attack` (Conquered, or Sacked when a player captain holds at Aggressive, which plunders inside the same dealing; the rival's bands are always Aggressive, so its retakes stay Conquered); `OrderSpec::Demand` (a parley comparing shown strength with shown defence; Submitted towns pay tribute and are watched by the hour, satisfied by an army, a garrisoned neighbour within 80 tiles or your sphere, and defy their lord after two weak days); courting (new `Gift` and `Defended` deeds, envoys from towns inside your sphere, and `Forsaken` when an invited town's drive-off request lapses; `Defended` never pays for fighting that town's own bandits); `OrderSpec::Subvert` (an agent, a real villager follower who goes to live in a rival town and is never recruited or sent to fight, whispers through no-spread deeds, raises a stir term, turns a fed town unvisited in about six days and is caught deterministically when suspicion reaches 1.0, about ten days unvisited; a turned town invites you through the existing `OfferKind::Invitation`).
4. **Influence.** A per-settlement `Influence` is recomputed hourly from held towns' people and loyalty with a linear distance falloff, as shares of player and rival against a town's own independence. It acts only through existing seams: a daily `Deed::Influence` (no spread), captain-free invitations in your sphere (at most one a day), and a contest loyalty term at owned towns. `Deed::spreads()` exempts `Influence` and `Whisper` from word of mouth, so nothing counts twice. Power is people and loyalty only (no specialty or building multipliers). The rival's seat is never swayed, but its defence bonus now follows the share of the rival's starting people that it still holds (`RivalState.realm`, a floor of 0.4 to 0.5, exactly 1.0 at a full realm), so every rival town you take, turn or free weakens the last assault. This amends ADR 0013's rival clock.
5. **Fallout.** A player conquest or sack queues a band of the town's own villagers (`Fallout`), spawned in the progress driver (never inside the orders step) as a RAIDERS band with a `Bandits` marker, capped at two bands. Bandits prefer the rival's towns and weakly held ones; the director's own bands keep their player-only prey and don't count bandits toward `max_bands`. In a run, `combat::succeed` passes `Raider` and `Bandits` to an heir. Killing a bandit posts `Deed::Killing` at his home town, as a militiaman's death does, so the fallout cannot be farmed for goodwill. The rival vows a reprisal when you take a town in its shadow or catch your agent: `RivalState.reprisal`, launched by `rival::clock` through the existing `raid()` with a new stream key, pinned to your newest holding by `RivalBand.pinned`, and counted in its garrison books.
6. **Governing.** `CommandKind::Govern { settlement, act }` sets three dials per town (work focus, dues, table), orders a building, appoints a steward or hands a dial back. Edicts live in a per-town `Edicts` component read at the point of use, never in `FactionModifiers` or the data. Every default is an exact no-op. A change starts within the game hour (the dial turns and locks, the first wave of moves, the first muster, the ration, the feast; later waves at dawn): work moves real villagers between trades (`sim_agents::jobs::reassign`); tithes and levies are a dealing (`orders::lands`) inside `run_orders`, collected by owner armies within reach in the hour you set them and then daily; rationing is a marker read in `update_needs` and `FoodSecurity`.
7. **Stewards are personalities, not optimisers.** Every owned run town has a named resident steward, chosen deterministically by trade, whose traits come from their own life (their trade, their town, their rank) and are visible habits, data rows of (dial, setting, condition) in progression.ron: a Stern steward levies and rations, a Forge-wise one forges even when hungry, a Husband never forges and feasts, a Reeve tithes, a Wright walls, a Native never levies. Habits set only the dials they touch and the player hasn't set; every other dial stays at its no-op default. The pending habit is computed a dawn ahead and shown with an Override. There are no hidden yield multipliers. A follower may be appointed instead, which costs the army that man.
8. **Building orders.** `sim_economy::choose_site` and `start_building` spawn a real site with `Construction`, supplied by carters' haul jobs and finished on a timer by the progress driver. M4b swaps `choose_site`'s body for master-plan plots and the timer for builders. New `WorkplaceKind`s, appended: `Granary`, `Palisade`, `Watchtower`.
9. **The smithy chain** is fixed in a run only (output-only fullness, capped input deliveries, dropping non-inputs, per-good haul minimums, runtime recipes). M4b removes the gate when it re-baselines the M2 tuning.
10. **Two new serial writes to one town's own stores:** a feast eats from its storehouse, and a finished site consumes its own stock. They follow `requests::pay`'s precedent and move nothing between two stockpiles.
11. **Schedule and formats.** Progress slots 13 (`influence::step`), 14 (`tenure::step`), 15 (`govern::step`) and 16 (`fallout::step`) are appended; M4c's planned `saga::settle` becomes slot 17. `SAVE_FORMAT` 2 and `REPLAY_FORMAT` 6, each bumped exactly once, in the step 1 skeleton; M4c's save format is one above M4a's final value.
12. **Event classification becomes explicit.** `notable()` is `(chronicled() && !quiet()) || RequestPosted`, with `EdictSet` and `Tribute` feed-only and `Forsaken`, `Built` and `StewardNamed` chronicled but quiet; no existing kind changes.
13. **Two inert exceptions reach every preset:** `Veteran.trade` (written in `enlist`, read only for stewards: state hashes change, behaviour and stdout do not) and `Without<Agent>` filters in `recruit_candidates` and `would_be_militia`.

## Consequences

- Towns differ by how they were taken, what they make and who runs them, and the panel can explain every number with a dated cause.
- ADR 0014's "a settlement changes hands only through `transfer`" still holds; `transfer_seeded` gains the tenure write and the fallout queue. Loyalty's target gains a sum of terms.
- ADR 0015's rule that per-town effects never enter `FactionModifiers` is applied to edicts, stewards, specialties and buildings: each is its own component, read where it acts.
- ADR 0018 keeps plunder storehouse-only and gains a granary's floor of 1.5 days.
- BUILD_GUIDE D2 holds for the director's bands; bandits deliberately prey on the rival, which adds noise to the rival's growth that `bot_run` and `tests/rival.rs` watch.
- M4 saves and replays are refused. M4c's numbering moves: its ADR becomes 0021, its `SAVE_FORMAT` 3, its slot 17.
- Run behaviour changes by design (stewards' habits set dials, specialties reshape populate, bandits and reprisals, the seat's defence follows its realm), so `bot_run` gates every step that touches a run, now with a win-day floor and comparisons against an Attack-only bot and a no-override bot, because a floor on wins alone cannot notice runs becoming trivial; the developed world, the skirmish and `--battle` stay byte-identical.

## Rejected

- **Influence as a per-tile sim field.** Per-settlement values are enough for every rule; the renderer draws the field from them.
- **Per-town bonuses in `FactionModifiers`.** It is faction-wide and rebuilt hourly; folding a town's edicts into it would leak them to every town.
- **Tithes delivered anywhere in your sphere.** That teleports goods between stockpiles outside the logistics step; armies within reach collect instead, and M4b's carters may carry later.
- **Rolls for discovery, stewards and vow times.** Deterministic thresholds shown to the player read better and need no new streams.
- **Bandit-held towns.** Bandits never `Attack`, so RAIDERS never become an owner.
- **A realm-wide default edict.** Stewards' habits already act on the dials the player leaves alone.
- **Stewards as optimisers with quiet yield bonuses.** They would make governing optional: a player ignoring every panel would get nearly the same realm. Habits that are visible and sometimes against the player's interest make appointing, overriding and releasing real decisions.
- **Influence multipliers for market towns and watchtowers,** and bare multipliers for fishing and farming specialties: numbers with no visible choice.
- **A posture-keyed sack for the rival.** Its bands are always Aggressive, so it would sack every retake and change its books; the sack is the player's rule.

## As built

### Step 1: foundations and the "why" (lane A)

- **The skeleton.** `CommandKind::Govern { settlement, act: GovernAct }` (tag 7) with the sim_core payloads `GovernAct`, `WorkFocus`, `Dues`, `Table`, `BuildKind` and `Dial` (positional tags, names); `OrderSpec::Demand` (11) and `Subvert` (12); `Cause` 15-23 (`Sack`, `Submission`, `Subversion`, `Defiance`, `Fallout`, `Reprisal`, `Edict`, `Caught`, `Deposed`); `SimEventKind` 22-33 (`Submitted` ... `Tribute`) with `quiet()` and `notable() = (chronicled() && !quiet()) || RequestPosted`; `Deed` 8-15 with `Deed::spreads()`. Validation refuses `Govern`, `Demand` and `Subvert` with "not yet" until their steps land. Every new type is hashed and saved: `Tenure`/`TakenBy`, `Influence`, `Fallout`, `Bandits`, `Steward` (sim_military); `LoyaltyTerms`, `DeedLog`, `Agent`, `Edicts`, `Rationed`, `Mustered`, `Assignment`, `Specialty` (sim_agents); `Construction` (sim_economy); `RivalState.realm`, `reprisal`, `last_reprisal`, `RivalBand.pinned`, `Veteran.trade`. `setup_run` inserts the per-town ones (`Tenure`, `Influence`, `LoyaltyTerms`, `DeedLog`, `Edicts`); the rest have no instance until their step, and `every_lordship_type_round_trips` (save_load) saves and loads one of each so their layouts are fixed under `SAVE_FORMAT` 2. `CommandKind` (and so `Govern`) is replay-only, as every variant before it: commands are carried by replays as RON (by variant name), never hashed or saved (`CommandLog` and `CommandScript` are exempt from the hash, and a save holds no pending commands, ADR 0016); its payloads that outlive a command (`WorkFocus`, `Dues`, `Table`, `BuildKind`, `Dial`, inside `Edicts` and `Assignment`) are hashed and saved by position. "Tag 7" is its declaration position. (CLAUDE.md and ADR 0016 list `CommandKind` among the saved enums; it is not one, though keeping it append only costs nothing.)
- **Formats.** `SAVE_FORMAT` 2 and `REPLAY_FORMAT` 6, bumped once for the milestone.
- **The cause-to-kind map** is `conquest::taking_kind`; `transfer_seeded` reports `Revolt` and `Defied` with the old owner as faction, counts only `Conquest` (conquest or sack) in `conquests`, and credits the champion for every kind but `Revolt` and `Defied`. The run summary counts `Submitted` and `Subverted` as takes and `Defied` as a loss.
- **Tenure and the why for real.** `transfer_seeded` writes `Tenure` through `tenure::record_taking` (the scar from progression.ron `tenure` and the blood the taker spilt, `Tenure::blood_of`; blood then starts again). `combat::casualties` counts each militiaman of the town killed for the faction he fought (`Tenure::spill`: `blood` with `blood_by`, a kill by another faction starting the count again, rebels counting for nobody), so a conquest after a raid never reads the raiders' blood, as the design's "militia killed in the taking" means; step 6's broken men must read `blood_of(taker)` before `record_taking` resets it. A taking rebuilds the town's `LoyaltyTerms` at once (`tenure::rebuild_terms`), so the terms change in the same tick as the owner rather than up to an hour later. `rival::setup` writes `Granted` and records the realm, the seat left out (so the share reaches 0.0 and the seat's bonus `realm_floor` when the seat holds nothing else, design section 6.1); `rival::free_lands` writes `Freed`. `tenure::step` (slot 14) rebuilds `LoyaltyTerms` hourly (Resentment and Stirred from the tenure; Contest, Dues, Table and Steward from `influence::contest_term` and `govern::dues_term`, `table_term`, `steward_term`, stubs returning 0.0 until their steps) and fades scars on the day tick, except a scar taken in that very tick. `loyalty_target` takes the terms' sum as `bias`, added before the clamp, and returns the unbiased mean untouched when the bias is 0.0. `apply_goodwill` writes `DeedLog` where present.
- **Deviations from the design's data.** `tenure.defended` and `edicts.feast.deed` are not separate fields: they duplicated `goodwill_deeds.defended` and `goodwill_deeds.festival`, which `GoodwillDeeds::delta` reads. `market` lands with the other blocks (the plan's step 1 list omitted it). `spread_totals` already skips deeds that don't spread (inert until step 3 posts any).
- **The panel's "why" and where its rules live.** The settlement panel's "Why they feel this way" section (`crates/game_ui/src/panels/why.rs`, UI-11) is pure text over state the panel copies out: the tenure line ("Yours since day 6, taken by the sword (resentment fades in 6 days)"), the loyalty target term by term (`loyalty_parts` and the non-zero `LoyaltyTerms`, wrapped at 44 characters, with its direction against the current mean and a note when the terms are capped), goodwill toward you and the rival with the newest-first deed log (days from 1, "(hearsay)" for `heard`), and the revolt risk graded from a rising to low. The Contest term is named from the owner's side: "rival's pull" at your towns, "your pull" at the rival's. The two rules that decide what the risk line names are pure functions in sim_agents `loyalty.rs`, so the panel, the tests and the later Dawn line share them: `deciding_term` (the term whose removal alone would carry the target across `revolt_threshold`: Awe or Bond holding a town, Resentment tipping it) and `loyalty_pulls` (hunger, ill will and the negative terms, largest first). Neither is hashed or saved.
- **Not yet wired.** The design also has a revolt's chronicle words cite its two largest pulls and the Dawn line say when Awe or Bond decided a town. `SimEvent` has no field to carry the pulls at the moment of the revolt, and the Dawn line is the feed's (step 4), so both wait; the panel names them from step 1 and `tests/tests/tenure.rs` pins the rules (`a_term_is_named_when_it_decides`).
- **Tests.** `tests/tests/tenure.rs` plays the step on the run island of seed 42: `transfer_writes_tenure` (a real fight's blood, the scar, the hourly Resentment term, the 0.02 fade, every cause's `TakenBy`), `every_cause_maps_to_its_kind_and_counters`, `every_event_kind_is_classified` (all 34 kinds, written and read back through the feed and chronicle), `the_log_dates_and_merges`, `terms_sum_into_the_target` (including the day's drift toward the biased target), `a_term_is_named_when_it_decides`, `every_cause_is_worded` and `another_factions_blood_is_not_the_takers` (raiders' kills are not the player's blood). A scar taken on a day tick (tick 0 is one) is not faded in that tick (`a_scar_is_not_faded_on_the_tick_of_its_taking`); the scenarios still take towns from tick 1, as real play does.

### Step 2: the smithy chain (SIM-64; lane B, 2026-10-13)

Decision 9 as built, amending [ADR 0006](0006-utility-ai-and-decision-traces.md) and [ADR 0007](0007-economy-and-transfer-resolver.md) for runs only: every branch is behind `in_run` and economy.ron `haul.run_fixes` (`ai::run_fixes_on`), so the developed world, the skirmish and `--battle` print byte-identical output and `village_day` and `famine` are unchanged. The `haul` block is left out of `Economy`'s RON text (its hash and save) while it holds the shipped values, so no developed-world hash moved either.

- **ADR 0006 (the AI), in a run:** `work_state` counts only outputs as fullness (a workplace full of inputs is Ready); a worker at a workshop (a recipe with inputs) whose hands are too full for a cycle, in units or in the four slots, puts surplus inputs into the workplace and carries anything else to the storehouse first (`WorkState::Drop`, `act::blocking_good`; a farmer's full hands keep the M2 rule); a worker carries in half a load of a `min_load` good itself; carters collect `min_load` piles; a cycle under way stays Work and, if it has used inputs, scores `haul.cycle_hold` (2.0) times as high. `decide` reads which input deliveries haulers are on before the parallel pass (`RunFixes::claims`; the decision order changes nothing), and afterwards sends back, in SpawnId order, carters who took the same free delivery in the same tick; a hauler keeps a delivery it is on even if someone has since claimed it (`Me::hauling`). Workers fetching their own inputs don't wait for each other (one fetch at a time starved the smiths: 29 weapons instead of 40).
- **ADR 0007 (goods), in a run:** an input delivery's pickup is capped at `input_target` cycles' worth minus the workplace's stock (`act::input_cap`, read in `perform` from the destination's `Stockpile`); at the storehouse, hands are emptied first before an input pickup (an interrupted haul's leftovers went to the smithy: 48 ore against a cap of 20) or a food fetch (weapons went home). Goods still move only through `Transfers`.
- **Two faults beyond the design's five,** found measuring: a smith who left a cycle half done (for a meal, or for a haul `work_state` offered mid-cycle) threw away its inputs and its time; on seed 1, about 145 weaponsmithing cycles started in three days and 40 weapons came out. Hence the mid-cycle rule and `haul.cycle_hold` (a new field beside the design's three).
- `sim_economy::set_recipe` (refuses a recipe made elsewhere) and `layout_recipe` (the layout's alternation, which leaving Forge restores) are public for steps 4 and 8.
- Measured (three days, no commands): seed 1 from 10 weapons (all at forges or in hands) to 68 (63 in storehouses); seed 7 from 0 to 20. Food security rose on both seeds (carters no longer haul 50-unit carts of one input) and mean hunger is unchanged. See `docs/m4a/STEP2.md`.

### Step 3: influence (lane A, sim)

- **The field.** `influence::power` (people and loyalty only, less the scar, the rival's seat times `seat_power`), `pull` and `field_at` are pure; `field_at` takes any iterator of `Source { id, at, faction, power }`, so the Demand watch (step 5) leaves a town out by filtering, and the renderer builds sources from each town's `Influence.power` (`Source::of`). `influence::step` (slot 13) refreshes every run town's `Influence` in SpawnId order; on the day tick it posts `Deed::Influence` (`warmth`: `goodwill_per_day x (net - warm_above)`, never past `goodwill_cap`) at towns the player doesn't own, the seat excepted; sets the envoy flags; and credits the champion. `influence.enabled: false` makes all of it, the Contest term, the envoys and the drifters' ease a no-op.
- **A taking refreshes the field.** `tenure::retake` calls `influence::refresh_field` (power and shares only) before it rebuilds the town's terms, so a town's Contest term follows its new owner in the same tick; otherwise the town's own power would count for its old owner for up to an hour (a conquered rival town would read the rival's lead, its own power included, until the next hour, and a daily drift in that hour would carry it). `Influence` is therefore written by slot 13 and at a taking (and slot 7 spends an envoy flag), never elsewhere.
- **The envoy flag lives on `Influence`** (`envoy: bool`, hashed and saved; the type is amended in place under `SAVE_FORMAT` 2). Slot 7 runs before slot 13, so the day tick flags up to `max_envoys_per_day` towns (independent, not the seat, net at least `envoy_net`, goodwill at least `invitations.goodwill` counting that dawn's own warmth, which the ledger applies on the next tick before slot 7 looks, nothing pending or cooling down; the highest net first, ties by SpawnId), and `invitations::step` posts a flagged town's invitation in the following hours when no captain is near. The flags are the day's budget: posting a flagged town's invitation spends its flag, and slot 7 reads no flag on the day tick itself (an unspent flag from the day before is stale there, since slot 13 sets the new day's after slot 7; a town flagged yesterday that came to like the player in the night would otherwise send envoys at dawn and the new day's town an hour later), so no counter is needed whatever `invitations.cooldown_days` says. An invitation by envoys is the ordinary `OfferKind::Invitation` with `Cause::Invitation`; the log says "sends envoys".
- **The heartland** (`+influence.heartland`) is a town of the player's where the player's share leads the rival's by at least `influence.sphere`; the Contest term is `-contest x (other - own)` where the other side leads (at either side's towns) and 0 between. A town's own power counts in its own shares. The heartland is the player's only (the design's "your heartland"): as first built it was given at either side's towns, which, with the seat's power, propped up nearly every rival town within about 120 tiles of the seat from tick 0 and would have undercut design section 5.4's subversion arithmetic (a fed rival town crossing 0.5 on day 6); the review of step 3 took it back.
- **The realm's rank credit is in slot 13** (the plan's "What"), not in `dominion::tally` (the design's list): `champion::credit_times(PLAYER, Feat::Realm, realm_units)`, with `Feat::Realm`'s points read from `influence.rank_points`. Player only: the rival's growth stays its clock.
- **The drifters' ease** reads the previous hour's `Influence` in slot 5: the goodwill line drops by `drifter_ease` where the net is at least `influence.sphere`.
- `spread_totals` and the deed log already skipped deeds that don't spread (step 1), so step 3 needed no change in `sim_agents::standing`.

### Step 3: influence, the map and the HUD (lane A, UI-12)

- **The overlay draws the sim's field.** `OverlayMode::Influence` ends the O cycle. `overlay::compute_overlay` copies each run town's `Influence` (power, shares, envoy flag), owner, people and goodwill into `overlay::influence::TownView`s every two seconds while the mode is shown (the other modes stay at half a second) and `InfluenceView::build` sums the same `field_at` over a grid of nodes 8 tiles apart. Shares are stored in 1/64, so equal grids tint identically: terrain reads the grid through `Overlay::tint` (bilinear between nodes) and `chunk_signature` (a chunk nobody pulls on keeps its no-field signature), and `Overlay.generation` moves only when the tint changes, never for a ring that fills. Render only reads: nothing here writes a sim entity, and leaving the mode drops the view.
- **What is drawn.** The tint runs from the player's faction colour through a front colour to the rival's, covering the terrain where either side pulls and fading out where nobody does. The front colour is amber, not the design's purple: army.ron's rival is purple already (0.55, 0.25, 0.7), and a purple front between blue and purple would not read as a third thing. The dashed sphere edges are the contour of net +`sphere` (yours) and -`sphere` (the rival's, an addition: the design draws only yours) cut from the grid by marching squares into dashes two tiles long with equal gaps, drawn as gizmos lifted off the terrain; rings (48 segments) go round every independent, non-seat town whose net is at least `warming`, filling with its goodwill over `invitations.goodwill`, brighter inside the sphere and gold on the day it sends envoys. The run bar adds a short legend in the mode. Nothing could be checked by eye: no GPU session ran here, so the screenshots of the plan's gate (RND frames for render steps) are still owed from a box with lavapipe.
- **The HUD.** The run bar's second line reads "Sphere N, warming M": N counts the towns (populated, yours included) whose net is at least `influence.sphere`, M the others that can warm to you (not yours, not the rival's seat, as the day's `Deed::Influence` goes) at least `influence.warming`; nothing when influence is off. The settlement panel's "Why they feel this way" section gains an influence line under the tenure line (`panels/why.rs`, `influence_lines`): the shares from the owner's side ("your pull 70%, the rival's 5%" with "your heartland" or "contested: the rival's pull leads"), and at a free town "you 60%, the rival 5%" with where it stands (warming, inside your sphere and how near its envoys are, on the front, inside the rival's sphere, out of reach). The seat reads "never swayed". A positive Contest term is named "heartland", not a pull.
- **Tests.** `tests/tests/influence.rs` plays the step on the run island (seed 42, one ten-day run on seeds 1 and 42 ignored): the field, the warmth, the contest and its effect on loyalty, envoys never two in a day and with no captain near, deeds that do not spread, the seat, the rank credit and the drifters; the overlay and panel have unit tests (docs/dev/TESTING.md). The done-when holds on the shipped tuning: a free town between two held towns invites with no army near on day 7 of seed 1 and day 8 of seed 42.

### Step 4: edicts, work, dues and table (GP-34, GP-17 part; lane B, 2026-10-13)

- **One write API, one applier.** `Edicts::order_work`, `order_dues` and `order_table` (sim_agents) ask for a setting; nothing else writes a dial. The player's `Govern` (the `Govern` arm of `orders::apply`, validated by `govern::refusal`: a run, your town, the dial unlocked, not already set or ordered, a feast the town can hold; Build, Steward and Release stay "not yet"), step 5's tribute (`order_dues(Dues::Tithe, false)`; `refusal` already keeps a submitted town's Dues at Tithe until `demand.settle_days` after its submission) and step 9's stewards all write through them. `govern::step` (slot 15) applies a pending setting at the next hour: the dial turns, it locks for `edicts.lock_days` from that hour, `EdictSet` is reported (cause `Player` or `Edict`), and the first wave, the first muster, the ration or the feast's meal go at once; later waves at each dawn (the day tick).
- **Edicts act only while you own the town.** A town that is not the player's has its non-default edicts reset in the next hourly step: recipes restored, the mustered released, the ration lifted, the feast ended; the run's building orders and the feast cooldown stay, and villagers an edict moved keep their trades (with their `Assignment`) until Balanced moves them back under your rule. The Dues and Table terms are 0.0 at any town not the player's.
- **Moves** go through `sim_agents::jobs` (`reassign`, `reassign_to`, `nearest_with_room`: populate's rule, nearest by squared distance from home, then workplace order; empty-handed and not mid-cycle only; carts resized; a Work or Haul under way ends so the AI decides again). A wave moves at most `ceil(moves_share x workers)`, at least `min_moves`, workers being the working-age civilian residents not enlisted, mustered or agents, in SpawnId order. Forge prefers a smithy with room, then whichever of the mine and the camp has fewer workers; Fields any food workplace with room; Walls carters (the storehouse, no slot limit, up to `walls_carters` assigned by Walls); Balanced moves the assigned back to `Assignment.from` (to idleness for one who was idle). A moved villager keeps his first trade in `from` and loses the `Assignment` when moved back to it. **Moves fill free places only:** workplaces keep their economy.ron slots, so a town whose smithies, mine and camp are nearly full gets few smiths from Forge (on seed 42's town #0, 5 in the first wave and none after); step 8's ordered smithy is the way to more places.
- **Dues are dealings of the logistics step.** `orders::lands` ends `run_orders` on the tick after each hourly step (run only), for each player town on Tithe or Levy whose dial turned in that hour or at the day tick: the Tithe's food beyond `tithe.keep_days` (pantry, storehouse first, at most the baggage room) and the storehouse's weapons and tools go to the nearest player captain within `edicts.reach` with room, which then arms its followers; the Levy's mustered (`muster` in `govern::step`: `levy.per_day` of the people, rounded, at least one, idle first, `levy.cap` waiting at most) are enlisted into the nearest player captain within reach, a garrison at the town included. Nothing moves without a collector. One `Tribute` event and log line per receiving captain; the feed groups a day's into one Dawn line.
- **Per-town effects are components read at the point of use,** never `FactionModifiers`: `Rationed { hunger, work }` (on the settlement for `FoodSecurity`, on residents for `update_needs` and `perform`; the numbers are copied from progression.ron when the ration starts, since sim_agents can't read sim_military's data; the type was a unit in step 1's skeleton and is amended in place under `SAVE_FORMAT` 2, as is `Edicts.feast_work`), `Mustered` (the AI never works and idles on the green), the feast (`Edicts.feast_until`, `feast_work`: work scores x0.3, idlers on the green), and Walls (`update_threats` x `walls_threat`, `resolve_strikes` + `walls_defence` to the militia within `garrison.radius`, `govern::build_speed` for step 8). Two serial writes to a town's own stores (decision 11): the feast eats from its storehouse in `govern::step`.
- **Defaults are exact no-ops.** A town at its defaults is never written; every consumer is a branch on a component's presence or a dial's value. `tests/tests/edicts.rs::defaults_are_no_ops` runs a run with and without `Edicts` and finds the same state hash.
- **The exhausted worker (step 2's open issue),** in a run with the smithy fixes: a worker at rest at or below `perform`'s 0.1 line no longer scores Work he can't start (the M2 rules looped Work, Done all day); by day his Sleep reads the evening's time factor, so he naps to 0.6 and goes back to work. Hauls need no rest and are unchanged.

### Step 5: taking towns: sack, demand, court (lane A, GP-32)

- **The sack is the player's rule.** `orders::attack` sacks at the hold's completion only for a player captain at Aggressive in a run (`orders::sack`, before `transfer` with `Cause::Sack`): the storehouse's food (keeping nothing) and its weapons and tools go into the baggage as far as the baggage's room allows, with `Deed::Plunder` and a log line. Every other conquest, the rival's always-Aggressive bands included, stays `Cause::Conquest` and plunders nothing (`a_rival_retake_is_conquered_not_sacked`).
- **The parley.** `OrderSpec::Demand` is the player's, in a run (the town has a `Tenure`), at an independent or rival town other than the seat, not during a refusal's cooldown. `orders::demand` marches the captain within army.ron `recruit.radius`, parleys `demand.parley_hours` (straying out of reach starts it again; an army at war with the captain's faction within `threat.radius` of the centre breaks it off, never a monument's guard camp, which never leaves its post), then compares with the pure `tenure::parley` (the same numbers the menu shows): `transfer_seeded(.., Cause::Submission, Some(demand.seed))` and the tribute, or `Deed::Threat`, `Tenure.cool_until` and the log line with the numbers. `conquest::would_be_militia` is `pub(crate)` and reads a `&World` (a `try_query`), so the forecast functions are read-only and the UI can call them.
- **Tribute through step 4's write path** (as merged at MP2; the lane first wrote the dial directly). `tenure::hold_tribute` orders the Dues dial to Tithe with `Edicts::order_dues(Dues::Tithe, false)` when the town kneels, and `tenure::step` re-orders it hourly while it pays if the dial is neither at Tithe nor ordered there. `govern::step` (slot 15, after the watch's slot 14) turns it at the hour like any setting: the ordinary `edicts.lock_days` lock, an `EdictSet` with `Cause::Edict`, and the first tithe in that hour to a player captain within `edicts.reach` (`orders::lands`), usually the army at the gate. The tribute lock is `govern::refusal`'s: Dues other than Tithe are refused ("tribute: held at Tithe") until `demand.settle_days` after the submission, after the dial's own lock ends. The Dues term is 0 while it pays, written in `tenure::rebuild_terms` whatever `govern::dues_term` says. A defiance writes no dial: the town is no longer the player's, so `govern::step`'s reset in the same pass lets the dial go (default, unlocked).
- **The watch** (`tenure::watch`, slot 14): strength within `watch_radius` against `hold_ratio x` its defence counted from its able adults only (the player is its garrison now); a town of the player's within the radius with `watch_garrison` soldiers; the player's other towns' influence share (`field_at` over every held town's `Source` but the town itself; never with influence off). `Tenure.watched_hours` counts the hours; on the day tick the day just ended is judged, but **not the day it knelt in** (a partial day would make a town taken at dusk defy a day early). Two weak days and it defies (`Cause::Defiance`); the player's soldiers within `garrison.radius` then face its militia for `garrison.revolt_hours` (its `Threat` provoked against the player, no revolt clock: it is free). It settles at `since + settle_days` (`tenure::settles_at`, `paying_tribute`), logged once; `how` stays `Submitted`.
- **Awe** is written in `rebuild_terms` while the town pays tribute and is watched this hour (computed by `watch`, once an hour, by `step`; a taking computes it at once).
- **Courting.** `tenure::gift` (from the `Give` arm, run only): `min(gift_per_day_of_need x days of need given, max_gift)`, then `x hungry_mult` when food security is under `requests.food_days` (the cap applies before the doubling, the design's order), once a town a day (`Tenure.gift_day`). `Deed::Defended` (in `tenure::step`, before the per-town pass) reads the hour's `BattleReport`s: a player report with a kill against raiders or the rival, its captain within `threat.radius` of an independent or player town. Once a town a day (`Tenure.defended_day`). An answered drive-off there (`requests::complete` → `tenure::drove_off`) spends the day's defence instead of tracking "this hour" (no new state; the town already got the request's +0.2), and at a sworn town counts one promise kept. Never against bandits whose home is the town: one of them fell since the battle's first blow (`Tenure.kin_fell`, the tick + 1, written in `combat::casualties` from the fallen man's band's `Bandits`, so a band wiped out to the last man still counts), or a living band of that home stands within `threat.radius` of the battle.
- **Forsaken.** `requests::fail` of a drive-off calls `tenure::forsaken`: at an invited town still the player's, `Deed::Forsaken` (spreads), `Tenure.broken`, a `Forsaken` event (cause `Lapsed`, amount the count) and the Bond turned at `forsaken_after`.
- **State.** `Tenure` gains `gift_day` and `defended_day` (day + 1, 0 never) and `kin_fell` (tick + 1, 0 never; hashed and saved, amended in place under `SAVE_FORMAT` 2). Nothing else new.
- **The card's words** come from `invitations::sworn_terms` (the seed `default_seed` gives and `tenure.bond`).
- **Open at step 5** (docs/m4a/STEP5.md): the run island's towns stand about 100 tiles or more apart, so the garrisoned-neighbour watch never applies at the shipped `watch_radius` 80; and the bot, demanding instead of attacking, wins every `bot_run` seed before day 14 (median 8.2), breaking the plan's win-day floor. Both are left for a decision at MP2 (decided: "Merge point 2" below).

### Merge point 2: tuning decisions (merge lead, 2026-10-13)

Step 5 left two questions open; the merge lead decided them at MP2 (data, in progression.ron `demand`; docs/m4a/STEP5.md "Open issues"):

- **A stiffer demand: unarmed men count half.** New field `demand.unarmed_weight`, shipped 0.5 (it was a fixed 1): a soldier's strength is `armed_weight` (1.5) armed, `unarmed_weight` unarmed. One rule, `tenure::soldier_strength`, serves the parley (`tenure::parley`), the right-click menu's "need N within 40, have M" (which reads `parley`), the bot's demand forecast (`bot::demand_target`) and the watch's strength (`tenure::watch` through `strength_near`): the design names a single "your strength" for both, so they stay one number. Why: with recruits counting in full, the bot made two to five towns kneel with an unbled band of fresh recruits and assaulted the seat early (every `bot_run` seed won before day 14, median 8.2). A demand now needs a real, armed army; arming first (the smithy chain, tithes, the bot's arms stop) pays. The win-day floor itself belongs to step 6 (the seat follows its realm): at a full realm the seat must hold until day 14 or later, and taking or turning its towns brings it down.
- **The watch reaches 125 tiles** (`demand.watch_radius`, was 80). The run island's towns stand about 100 tiles or more apart (seed 42: nearest neighbours 101 to 152 tiles), so at 80 the design's "a garrisoned neighbour does as well as an army parked beside it" never applied. The radius also widens the strength check of the watch. `take_modes::a_garrisoned_neighbour_or_a_sphere_keeps_it_watched` now runs on the shipped value (it set 125 itself before).
- **Courting** still never fires in the bot's runs while Demand qualifies first; the stiffer demand helps, and step 10's bot tuning owns the "bot courts at least one town" check of `bot_uses_lordship`.

### Step 6: fallout: bandits and reprisals (lane A, GP-33, GP-31)

- **One queue, one spawner.** `transfer_seeded` calls `fallout::queue_taking` after `tenure::record_taking`, which now returns the taker's blood read before the taking resets it; the rival's influence share is read before the field refresh. A player's conquest or sack queues `PendingBand` and stamps `Fallout.last_violent`; a player's take of any kind from the rival or under its shadow queues `PendingVow` (`Fallout.vows`, new). Only slot 16 (`fallout::step`) acts on the queue. Both new fields (`vows`, `PendingBand.brooding`) amend the step 1 skeleton in place under `SAVE_FORMAT` 2.
- **Broken men are its own people, captain included.** The band's captain is the first of the town's recruit candidates (enlisted, then made a RAIDERS captain with `Raider` and `Bandits`), not a spawned stranger: no `spawn_army`, no RNG, and the population drops by exactly the band. They gather at the town's edge (`threat.radius` + 10 toward the rival's seat), because rising inside the town got them cut down by its garrison within the hour.
- **Bandits' minds** are a run-only branch of `raiders::think` (`fallout::bandit_score`, the garrison read from the fighter grid, the player's share from `Influence`); the director's own bands keep BUILD_GUIDE D2's player-only prey, and `director::step` counts `Without<Bandits>`. `combat::succeed` carries `Raider` and `Bandits` to an heir in a run only (outside one the M3 behaviour, and so the skirmish and battle outputs, are unchanged). A bandit slain by a player's blow posts `Deed::Killing` at his home (`Casualties.slain_by`).
- **Reprisals ride the raid path.** `rival::split_band` is the raid's old body with a stream key and an optional pin; a reprisal uses `sim_military::rival::reprisal`, the only new stream. It counts in `RivalState.raids`, so the garrison's books balance as before (`the_garrison_books_include_reprisals`). The band is pinned to `rival::newest_holding` at launch; a retake is the rival's ordinary `Attack` (`Cause::Conquest`, `Conquered`, never a sack). A vow waits while it can't launch; the next one (after the cooldown) replaces it.
- **The seat follows its realm.** `rival::realm` counts the starting people of `RivalState.realm`'s towns the rival still owns; `seat_bonus = defence_on(day) x strength x realm_factor`, where `realm_factor` returns exactly 1.0 at a full realm by a branch (`floor + (1 - floor) x 1.0` is not bit-exact in f32). `a_full_realm_is_an_exact_no_op` pins it.
- **The seat calibration (MP2's open floor).** With the realm now scaling the bonus, the rival rows' `defence_steps` were raised so an intact realm holds (steady and chaotic `(0, 1.5) (5, 2.0) (10, 2.25) (20, 2.5) (30, 2.75)`, harsh `(0, 1.75) ... (24, 3.25)`): an untouched seat is about 2.5-6x the old clock, an emptied one (at `realm_floor` 0.4) about the old clock. `bot_run` returns to the win-day floor (docs/m4a/STEP6.md); the final assault follows the sphere war, as section 6.1 intends.
- **Pauses.** director.ron `auto_pause` gains ReprisalVowed and Defied on steady and chaotic; a bandit rising does not pause (the design's three pausing kinds).

### Step 9 (specialties): towns that differ (SIM-62; lane B, 2026-10-13)

Built in the gap before the smoke-playtest verdict (MP2), without construction or stewards. Notes: [docs/m4a/STEP9a.md](../m4a/STEP9a.md); the balance census that came with it: [docs/m4a/CENSUS.md](../m4a/CENSUS.md).

- **Read from the site, at populate, in a run only.** The branch needs both a sized run island and a `RunConfig` (`RunState::in_run`), so the developed world, the skirmish and the battle never take it (their stdout is byte-identical). Each town takes the first matching row of villagers.ron `specialties` (`population::specialty_of`). A row matches on its tiles and its roads. Tiles are reachable tiles of an economy.ron site rule within `radius` (40) of the centre, counted on the bare map before anything is built (`sim_economy::site_tiles`, with `build_settlement`'s own site test and reach). Roads are worldgen roads that end at the town (`population::road_degree`). The row's multipliers scale the occupation shares before `apportion`. `Specialty` goes on the settlement there (not in `setup_run`), and the counts go in `PopulationReport.sites`.
- **The draws do not change; the layout does** (BUILD_GUIDE G10). `apportion` and the job shuffle draw on the job count, which is the town's workers either way, so every town's populate stream yields the same number of words. `PopulationReport.draws` records them (`ChaCha8Rng::get_word_pos`), and `populate_draws_are_unchanged` compares them with and without specialties on seeds 1, 7 and 42. The scaled counts change how many workplaces each town builds, which shifts the shared `Occupied` set and later SpawnIds on the run island only.
- **Fishing and Timber thresholds lowered from the design's 400 and 600 to 160 coast and 200 forest-edge tiles.** Those sites are edges, so within 40 tiles they run to about 270 and 340 on the run island, and the design's rows never matched (seeds 1 and 7 had no Fishing or Timber town). At 160 and 200 each island has one or two of each, behind Mining and Market in row order (seed 1: 4 Mining, 6 Market, 2 Fishing, 1 Timber, 12 Farming; seed 7: 3, 8, 1, 1, 12).
- **Data, kept out of the hash while shipped.** `VillagerData.specialties` (`SpecialtyRule`: `kind`, `site`, `min_tiles`, `road_degree`, `radius`, `shares`) defaults to `SpecialtyRule::shipped()` and is skipped from the RON text while it equals it, following `haul`'s precedent (step 2). A unit test keeps the file and the code equal. So the developed world's hash and saves did not move. An empty list turns specialties off and is written and read back. Scenario tests that only moved with the layout opt out through `common::run_app_without_specialties`, which uses the new `sim_headless::headless_app_with` (an edit to the world after the plugins build and before populate).
- **One visible flow each.** Mining arms you through the smithy chain: miners x4 and smiths x2, so the mine keeps pace with a Forge (CENSUS: 8 to 39 weapons a day on Forge on its own ore, where a farming town's starting ore lasts 2 to 5 days). Market posts hired men: `govern::market_hire`, the end of slot 15, on every hire hour (`market_hire_due`: every `market.hire_days` rounded to whole hours, from tick 0, never at tick 0). At each Market town the player owns, in SpawnId order, it camps a band of `market_band` men (`people / people_per_man`, `min_men` to `max_men`) `hire.distance` tiles from the centre, in a direction set by the golden angle times its SpawnId (no draw, no new stream). It then posts the band's `Hire` offer with `post_offer`. The band is the start's sellswords' band (`sellswords::camp`, factored out of `setup` without changing it), so hiring, declining and lapsing go through the existing `answer` and `lapsed`. No new state: the schedule is the clock, and the band is captains and followers already hashed and saved. Timber's half-price works are step 8's (`construction` reads `Specialty::Timber`). Fishing and Farming are larders, made of people only.
- **UI** (lane C's files, built here by a Sonnet agent): a `[Mining]` tag line under the label's name (`labels::specialty_tag`) and a panel line saying what it gives (`panels::specialty_line`).
- **Balance.** No data was changed by the census. Its proposals for step 10 are in CENSUS.md: Forge's 12 to 25 weapons a day per smithy is above the 8 to 12 target, so weaponsmithing should slow in a run only if `bot_run` falls under its floor; a full Mining town's Forge moves nobody without step 8's places.


### Merge point 3: a decision's cost and the bot's count (MP3 fixer, 2026-10-13)

The MP3 gate (docs/m4a/MP3.md) was red on NFR-11: on the botless seed-7 run's live ticks the combined tree cost +5.9% over the M4 binary, nearly all of it in Decide (step 2's two serial passes over every villager in `ai::decide`, a little more per decision, and step 9's layout). The fix is code only: **no data changed**, and every hash is unchanged (the seed-42 run replay and the seed-4 and seed-3 bot replays reproduce their gate hashes in both threading modes; the developed, skirmish and battle stdout is byte-identical), so `bot_run` and every scenario test play exactly as at the gate.

- **The idle spot is drawn only when Idle wins** (amends [ADR 0006](0006-utility-ai-and-decision-traces.md) in cost only). Every decision drew its Idle option's wander spot, a fresh ChaCha8 stream per villager per decision (about a fifth of the cost of scoring), though Idle seldom wins. The Idle candidate now carries its anchor (home or the green) and `AiWorld::goal_of` draws the spot for the chosen option only. The draw is keyed by villager and tick (`sim_agents::idle_spot`), so the spot is the same whenever it is drawn.
- **The trace's best three without a sort.** `ai::top_three` picks the first three of the stable sort the trace used (score, then kind) without allocating; `top_three_matches_a_stable_sort` pins the equivalence, ties and NaN included.
- **Step 2's second pass is gone.** The carters who take a free input delivery are noted as they choose, inside the parallel pass (a mutex they rarely touch), then sorted by SpawnId; only the losers are fetched and sent back. The claims pass before the decisions stays (it must see the tick's start) and, like `resolve_transfers`' gathering of orders, folds over its query (`for_each`) instead of stepping it, which takes Bevy's dense-table path; `resolve_transfers` had come to cost twice M4's per agent on the combined tree and now costs less than M4's.
- **Measured.** Instructions per tick (callgrind, every thread, ticks 2 to 4,800 of the botless seed-7 run): M4 7.90 million, the gated tree 8.34 million (+5.5%), the fixed tree 7.68 million (-2.9%). Wall clock, live ticks 1 to 4,800, 48 interleaved pairs in two sessions: **-0.1% against M4** (paired mean; 95% interval -3.6% to +3.4%; median of medians +0.4%), Decide -3.5% and Act -2.8% against M4; against the gated tree in a three-way run, Decide -11.5% (t = -5.2) and the total -2.9%.
- **The bot's count** (`sim_headless::bot::Takes`, reporting only). A retake is now the first change of hands of the town a `RivalReprisal` named after the band was sent, counted once per reprisal, with how many the reprisal band itself made ("retaken N (by its band M)"); the gate's count took any rival conquest of any town a reprisal ever named, before the vow included. A new column counts the player's towns the rival conquered by whose band took them ("lost to the rival: raids R reprisals P other O"; an heir, who has no event of his own, counts as other), which is where most lost towns come from.
